fp. 10 Representation vs symbolic representation

But … a version of the old opposition between matter and mind persists. It persists in the way we study brain and mind, excluding as "peripheral" the roles of the rest of the body and the local environment. It persists in the tradition of modeling intelligence as the production of symbolically coded solutions to symbolically expressed puzzles.

This old opposition is the implicit bad guy of the picture which we are about to overcome. I agree that symbolic representations (i.e. in language or language like symbols) are not the be-all and and end-all [or even the foundation] of cognition/reasoning. However, are we setting up to throw the representational baby out with the symbolic bathwater?

(fp. 11):

to abandon the idea of neat dividing lines between perception, cognition, and action

I think my view aligns neatly with this

introduction

very first paragraph (fp. 15):

Where are the artificial minds promised by 1950s science fiction and 1960s science journalism? Why are even the best of our "intelligent" artifacts still so unspeakably, terminally dumb? One possibility is that we simply misconstrued the nature of intelligence itself. We imagined mind as a kind of logical reasoning device coupled with a store of explicit data—a kind of combination logic machine and filing cabinet.

This has aged, if not poorly, then at least strangely. It's not just that AI (or LLMs) have had an unexpected breakthrough. Its that this breakthough has been achieved by the very disembodied, inactive, purely language focused approach that Clark spends the next five pages decrying. Clark might argue (and I might agree) that the flaws of these LLMs (hallucinations, ludicrous errors etc.,) are ineliminable using current techniques, and are the result of the language focused approach he is complaining of. He is also right that this breakthrough was, at least partly, the result of abandoning attempts to encode logical (or even probabilistic) inferential relations between sentences (and instead focusing on the statistical relations between linguistic tokens in the streams produced by competent speakers). Still, if the failures of the old language-centric attempts to build "intelligent" artifacts counted against that approach, then surely its successes must count for it.

More specifically, (fp. 17):

CYC looks set to become a bigger, fancier, but still fundamentally brittle and uncomprehending "expert system." Adding more and more knowledge to CYC will not remedy this.

This is kind of the most shocking thing. All the clever people developing clever theories about minds and cognition didn't get that far. The successes of LLM's are not, primarily, due to the cleverness of their training algorithms (though there is room for some). The real driver of their successes is the huge quantity of data that has been fed into them. Just Adding more and more knowledge, more and more strings of symbols, succeeded in producing flexible, responsive and often reasonable conversational partners.

1.3 minds without models

So there are at least two distinct themes (morals) that Clark extracts from the parade of robots that takes up the beginning of the books body:

The first is lack of symbolic representation, the second is the lack of a central planner. Clark explicitly links the two:

The trouble with the central planner is that is profoundly impractical. It introduces what Rodney Brooks aptly termed a "representational bottleneck" blocking fast, real-time response. The reason is that the incoming sensory information must be converted into a single symbolic code so that such a planner can deal with it. And the planners' output will itself have to be converted from its propriety code into the various formats needed to control various types of motor response. These steps of translation are time- consuming and expensive.

lack of central planning

The lack of central planning, but rather a range of competing (or at least interacting) subsystems, is very very reminiscent of Dennett's account of mental representational processes (see dennett2017-historyOfQualia and perhaps dennett1991-consciousnessExplained).
