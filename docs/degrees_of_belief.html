credences are connected to belief in probabilities

A certain coin is scheduled to be tossed at noon today. You are sure that this chosen coin is fair: it has a 50% chance of falling heads and a 50% chance of falling tails. You have no other relevant information. Consider the proposition that the coin tossed at noon today falls heads. To what degree should you now believe that proposition?

More generally, if one believes that there is a N% probability of E occurring, then ones confidence in the belief that E will occur simply is (barring confusion, limit cognitive resources etc.) N%.

Anyone who thinks there is a N% chance of E, but believes that E will occur with a different credence is either

  1. likely to have their expectations falsified (if they are right about the N% chance)
  2. unable to reason probabilistically (if their credence generates likely expectations)

dutch book

Apparently this line of argument is due to frank Ramsey, or at least he used it. The traditional argument is that it is irrational to hold beliefs with credences that allow a dutch book to be made against them. But the Dutch book is just a way of codifying that things do not go as the subject expects. They make each bet (supposedly) in the expectation of making a profit. In a dutch book we can demonstrate that those expectations are not fulfilled. Thus the appeal of having probabilities drop out altogether. But the point is really that one's expectations are likely to (or if there is a dutch book, are certain to) be mistaken.

It is so arbitrary to put numbers on our degrees of belief - how likely is there to be a red car on the street when I next walk along it? The numbers feel palpably arbitrary. I don't seem able to distinguish between believing such a claim with 70% confidence and with 72% confidence (let alone 72.5). I think things are pretty likely, very likely, near certain (perhaps beyond reasonable doubt? So certain that I need not bother checking further before acting?) or absolutely certain. But why no numbers?

Because we dont really model things we consider to be very unlikely. We dont waste the mental/neurological resources to model unlikely events. So say we actively model (develop expectations for) a range of expectations that we are confident will occur.
