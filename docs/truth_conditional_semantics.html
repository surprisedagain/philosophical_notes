truth conditional semantics

short

Semantic theories based upon the claim that meanings are truth conditions. now aka possible worlds semantics

related to

hub: influential_semantic_theories ++Fregean_semantics ++Russellian_semantics

main

The original naive version of this is sometimes expressed by early analytic philosophers (such as Russell) as the doctrine that the meaning of a sentence is its truth conditions. Or, to remain agnostic about the existence of meanings, sentences are synonymous iff they are true under the same conditions (or in the same possible worlds).

Consideration of indexicals and the realisation that the truth conditions of some sentences may vary depending upon their contexts of assertion and evaluation led to refinement and increased sophistication.

On the modern view: Assertions (i.e. sentences asserted in a given context of assertion) are synonymous iff they have the same truth-value with respect to every circumstance of evaluation.

Or as the Stanford Encyclopedia somewhat tendentiously puts it:

two sentences express the same proposition if and only if it is impossible for them to differ in truth-value.

(this is a modern version of Fregean_semantics#Freges criterion of synonymy hence that can be taken as a form of truth functional semantics - however senses complicate things)

A function from possible worlds to truth values is sometimes called an intension in more recent (quasi-technical) literature.

objections

all necessary truths synonymous

It seems implausible that all necessary truths assert the same proposition despite being true in the same possible worlds (i.e. all of them).

e.g. It seems that

  1. 2 + 2 = 4
  2. There are infinitely many prime numbers

have different meanings despite being true in exactly the same possible worlds (i.e. all of them).

propositional attitudes

propositional_attitudes If sentence meanings are also the objects of belief (i.e. they are both propositions) and the sentences (1) and (2) are synonymous, then the belief ascriptions:

Mary believes that 2 + 2 = 4 Mary believes that there are infinitely many prime numbers

should also be synonymous. But it seems possible for them to differ in truth value and hence they are not synonymous (according to truth conditional semantics itself). (but see Fregean_semantics#unknown synonyms for an attempted defence)
