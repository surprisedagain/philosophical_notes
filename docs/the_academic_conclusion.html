the academic conclusion

short

“More research required!”, which may be true, but is often used in place of a conclusion when nothing better can be reported.

main

The academic conclusion is easier to recognise than define. It the justification of research/investigation/publishing on the grounds that more questioning, more consideration of possibilities, more critical thinking is always a good idea.

Some examples may help us to get a sense of what we are talking about:

examples

One wonders if it is ever not worth exploring how the kinds of questions we ask are influenced. If not then the specification of “causality in medicine” is superfluous and gives a false sense of relevance. If Felicia isn't coming to dinner, so perhaps we shouldn't release the attack dogs into the nursery. But the academic conclusion is so vacuously acceptable that it requires such superfluous specification in order to appear to carry any weight at all.

The sense that merely considering a range of possibilities, opening up, or uncovering some detail of a historical account, is in and of itself a worthwhile accomplishment. In many current intellectual circles “problematising” is a term of praise.
