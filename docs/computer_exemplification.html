Occasionally I feel anxious that I will die before being able to explain semantic_holism sufficiently clearly to do this beautiful semantic principle justice. Of course, any philosopher worth their salt would be delighted to have good reasons for believing a principle that is at once so clear, profound, elegant, and (not least important) true, and yet so widely disbelieved. After all one of the chief joys of philosophy is the license it gives for largely unsuccessful attempts to foist one's opinions on more or less innocent passers by. The field is thus apt to attract those who enjoy the the painless suffering of being a lone voice in the wilderness, and I am no exception.

It would, therefore, be disingenuous to count the fact that semantic_holism and the accompanying doctrine of indeterminacy of translation are both widely held to be either false, self-contradictory or incoherent as a tribulation for those, like myself, who espouse the doctrine.
